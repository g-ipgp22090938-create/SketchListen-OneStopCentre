🎧 Say hello

Meet SketchListen

A listening game where pupils connect the dots to find a secret keyword.

📛

Its name

SketchListen: a listen-and-connect-the-dots activity for primary pupils who find listening hard.

👂

The skill

We practise listening: finding the main idea and the small details in a short story. (For teachers: Year 5 English, Content Standard 1.2 and Learning Standards 1.2.1, 1.2.2 and 1.2.3.)

📖

The topics

The sample story is “Amir and the Kite” (World of Stories). Teachers can make new lessons about their own topics, like celebrations, food or animals!

🤔 What is hard for pupils?

Audio plays once, in real time. You cannot pause it or go back, like you can with a book. Pupils who are still learning English use all their brain power just to work out the words, so there is little room left to remember the meaning. Then the questions come, and many pupils end up guessing. Guessing wrong makes them feel less brave, and the next listening task feels even harder.

🔎 How do we know?

Research says so

Satori (2021) studied 150 Japanese learners of English and found that memory was linked more strongly to listening in the lower-level group.

Not every study agrees

Luu (2024) found only a weak link among 65 Vietnamese beginners. So the picture is mixed, but both studies put memory at the centre of weaker listeners’ difficulties.

The curriculum

Year 5 pupils are expected to understand the main idea and specific details of short texts, with support (Learning Standards 1.2.1 and 1.2.2).


🎯 The gap we want to close

😟 Where pupils are now

They cannot hold on to what they hear, so they guess at the answers and lose confidence.

😄 Where we want them to be

They understand the main idea and the key details, and they answer with evidence and confidence.

Our aim: help low-level Year 5 listeners hold on to what they hear, by turning every short piece of audio into a picture and a keyword that pupils build themselves, and by giving teachers a template to make their own lessons.

💡 How the idea grew

⏯️

Idea 1: a pausable player

It gives thinking time, but pupils still keep everything in their heads.

🧭

Idea 2: a strategy platform

Good for planning, but weak listeners can’t reflect on audio they can’t decode yet.

✏️

The winning idea!

Combine both. The audio pauses by itself, keywords help, and pupils connect dots to build a picture that holds the meaning for them.

🛠️ What we made it with

  • A web app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript). It opens in any browser on a phone, tablet or computer, so it suits shared school devices and needs no installing.
  • Voice recording or audio upload. Teachers use their own voice, so pace and accent feel familiar to pupils.
  • A lesson file you can export and import. Lessons are easy to keep, share and reuse.

🎮 Let’s play

How to Play

Five easy steps for pupils, then how teachers make their own lesson. These screenshots come from the real product!

🧒 For pupils

1Press Start. You will see the lesson name and what we are practising.

2Listen carefully. Press Play and hear one short part of the story. The audio stops by itself.

3Connect the dots. Tap the numbers in order. A picture appears and the secret keyword is revealed! No device? Draw the keyword on paper and take a photo.

4Answer the questions. Your pictures stay at the bottom as clues, so you answer from what you know, not from a guess!

5See your score! Ticks show the right answers and your score pops up straight away. Then play again or go home.

🧑‍🏫 For teachers: make your own lesson

1Open the Teacher editor and set the lesson name, year and theme. Choose your options: show the keyword after pupils finish connecting and/or let pupils replay the chunk.

2Add your audio. Up to 6 chunks, each with one main idea and one keyword. Record your own voice or attach an audio file.

3Pick the picture to connect for each keyword: kite, tree, ladder, sun, house, bus, book, star, fish, boat, or your own object.

4Write your questions from your audio. Type three answers, tick the right one and link it to a chunk.

5Preview and export. Try it as a pupil, then export the lesson file. Another teacher can import it on their own device.

🚀 How it can help learners

These are hoped-for benefits from research. We still need to test them with Malaysian primary pupils.

🧠

Remember more

Short, paused chunks suit small working memory (Satori, 2021). The picture and keyword are a memory clue.

🎯

Guess less

Pupils answer with their picture clues in view.

💪

Feel braver

Seeing ticks and a score can make the next listening task feel less scary.

🔄

Stay active

Making visuals while learning helps memory (Xie et al., 2024), and a Listen-and-Draw approach improved listening (Nisa & Andriani, 2025).
